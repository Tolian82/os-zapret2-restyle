## Cross-domain discovery interpretation

Across the supplied three ten-candidate corpora, each cheap variant has:

- `29/29` agreement on comparable candidate pairs;
- zero recorded false PASS;
- zero recorded false FAIL;
- one additional Rutracker pair excluded from direct pass/fail comparison because the deep 16-KiB policy was `inconclusive` on a valid short response.

This is encouraging agreement evidence, especially because YouTube supplies a true deep-16K PASS case and Rutracker supplies a short-response connectivity case that deep validation cannot classify as 16-KiB PASS.

It is **not** yet accepted `_5` evidence because every live report has `cleanup_ok=false`. The Telegram direct cleanup follow-up was clean, making a systematic harness/finalization defect plausible, but the exact cause is not yet proven.

The timing evidence also does not show the material cost advantage required by the `_5` decision boundary:

- Telegram: `get-1` mean total was `9.5 ms` faster than current `get-4k` (`5464.8` vs `5474.3 ms`), only about `0.17%`;
- Rutracker: current `get-4k` was `7.0 ms` faster than `get-1` and `8.4 ms` faster than HEAD;
- YouTube: current `get-4k` was `10.5 ms` faster than `get-1` and `19.7 ms` faster than HEAD.

Because each domain contributes ten samples, the equal-sample pooled mean total time across all 30 samples is approximately:

- `head`: `3452.97 ms`;
- `get-1`: `3442.37 ms`;
- `get-4k`: `3439.70 ms`.

The current 4-KiB discovery probe is therefore the lowest-cost cheap variant in this supplied pooled set, but the differences are tiny relative to observed run-to-run/network variation. No cheaper replacement has demonstrated a material reproducible advantage.
